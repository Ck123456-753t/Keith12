From audio to a clean, professional transcript.

A transcript is more than words copied from a recording. It needs consistent speaker labels, accurate timestamps, readable punctuation, correct spelling, and a layout that clients can review without decoding a mess.

Formatting checklist

Core standards
01
Use a clear header

Include the transcript title, client ID when provided, company, transcriber, date, and recording duration.

02
Keep speaker labels consistent

Use labels such as Male_1 and Female_1 when names are unknown. Do not switch labels halfway through.

03
Place timestamps consistently

Use the required format, for example [00:00:08 --> 00:00:11]. Match the audio, not a guess.

04
Proofread punctuation

Use sentence boundaries, commas, apostrophes, and capitalization to reflect the speaker's meaning.

05
Follow the requested style

Clean verbatim and strict verbatim are different. Follow the client's instructions for fillers, repetitions, false starts, and grammar.

06
Finish cleanly

Use consistent spacing and the required closing footer. Never invent words to make unclear audio sound complete.

Important: “Verbatim” does not mean careless formatting. Preserve the spoken content according to the assigned style while applying the required layout and punctuation conventions.

What good formatting looks like

The goal is consistency and fidelity, not decorating every sentence until it looks like a wedding invitation.

Accurate, not guessed

Listen again when a word, name, or timestamp is uncertain. Mark unintelligible speech only according to the client’s rules.

Readable, not rewritten

Correct punctuation and capitalization, but do not silently change a speaker’s meaning or rewrite their words.

Consistent from start to end

Keep headings, speaker labels, timestamp style, spacing, and footer format uniform throughout the document.

Plain-text layout

A transcript can also be delivered in a simple document format. The same structure should remain clear even without visual styling.
