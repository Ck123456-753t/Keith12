How a professional transcript is formatted

Use this reference to understand the structure of a transcript: metadata, speaker labels, timestamps, speech paragraphs, and the closing footer. The example below is based on the transcript supplied for this guide.

Transcript metadataSpeaker labelsTimestampsPunctuationVerbatim accuracy

Formatting checklist

1Transcript title

Use a clear title that identifies the recording or case.

2Metadata block

Keep client ID, company, transcriber, duration, and date in a consistent order.

3Speaker labels

Keep labels such as Male_1 and Female_1 consistent. Do not swap speakers.

4Timestamp format

Use [HH:MM:SS --> HH:MM:SS] where required, and verify it against the audio.

5Speech and punctuation

Use readable sentence punctuation and paragraph breaks without changing the words spoken.

6Closing footer

Include the required end marker and company copyright/footer details.

Verbatim means preserving speech according to the assigned style. It does not mean ignoring the client’s formatting instructions, and it does not permit silently rewriting what the speaker said.

Three rules to remember

Formatting makes a transcript easy to read; it must never be used as an excuse to alter the source.

Accurate, not guessed

Replay unclear sections. Do not invent words, names, or timestamps to make a line look complete.

Readable, not rewritten

Apply punctuation and capitalization as required by the style guide, while preserving the speaker’s wording.

Consistent to the end

Use the same speaker-label format, timestamp style, spacing, and footer throughout.
