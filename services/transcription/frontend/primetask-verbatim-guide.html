Lesson 01

What is verbatim transcription?

Transcription is the process of turning spoken audio or video into written text. Verbatim transcription aims to preserve the words spoken, including meaningful repetitions, fillers, false starts, and incomplete sentences, according to the client's style guide.

The core rule: Do not rewrite the speaker to make them sound smarter, clearer, or more professional. Your job is to represent the recording accurately, not edit the speaker's personality out of existence.

What should you capture?

  • The words that can actually be heard.
  • Repeated words, fillers such as “um” or “uh,” and false starts when required by the brief.
  • Speaker changes and meaningful non-speech sounds when the assignment requires them.
  • Punctuation that makes the spoken meaning readable without changing it.

Always follow the client's instructions first. “Verbatim” is not one universal formatting standard, and style guides can differ.

Lesson 02

Know the transcript type

Strict / full verbatim

Preserves spoken wording closely, including fillers, repetitions, stutters, false starts, and relevant vocal events, as specified by the client.

Speaker: I, um, I thought we—we were leaving at six.

Clean verbatim

Preserves the speaker's meaning and wording while removing some disfluencies that the client says to omit. It does not authorize rewriting ideas.

Speaker: I thought we were leaving at six.

Edited / intelligent verbatim

May improve readability by removing repetitions or speech clutter and lightly adjusting wording under the agreed rules. It is not strict verbatim.

Time-coded transcript

Includes timestamps at the intervals or speaker changes specified in the instructions. Timestamps must match the media, not a guess.

Lesson 03

The complete workflow

  1. Read the brief. Confirm transcript type, spelling preferences, timestamp intervals, speaker labels, and delivery format.
  2. Listen once. Understand the topic, number of speakers, accents, and audio quality before typing everything.
  3. Draft in short sections. Replay difficult sentences. Use a slower playback speed if useful, without changing the original meaning.
  4. Identify speakers. Use the required labels consistently, such as Speaker 1 or Male_1. Do not guess a person's name or gender.
  5. Add timestamps. Use the requested format and verify every timestamp against the recording.
  6. Review with the audio. Listen again while following the text. Check omissions, repeated words, names, numbers, and sentence endings.
  7. Proofread and submit. Correct spelling and punctuation without silently changing what was said. Check the file name and required header/footer.
Useful habit: Separate drafting from proofreading. Trying to hear, type, punctuate, and edit every word at the same time is a fine way to miss all four.
Lesson 04

Formatting, punctuation, and timestamps

Suggested transcript header

Transcript title: [Accurate title] Client ID: [Provided ID, if applicable] Company: PrimeTask Workspace Transcriber: [Your assigned name] Duration: HH:MM:SS Date: [Required date format] Speaker 1: [00:00:00 --> 00:00:08] Exact words heard in this section.

Use the exact field names and order requested by your client. Never invent a client ID, speaker identity, date, or duration. If a field is not supplied, leave it blank or use the approved placeholder.

Timestamp basics

  • Use the required format, for example [00:00:08 --> 00:00:15].
  • Start and end times must reflect the audio segment accurately.
  • Do not create fake timestamps to make the page look complete.
  • Follow the client's rule on whether timestamps appear every interval, at each speaker turn, or at another specified point.

Punctuation and grammar

Use punctuation to represent the speaker's sentence structure and meaning. A verbatim transcript can still have correct capitalization and punctuation. Do not repair grammar by replacing the speaker's actual words with better ones.

Example: If a speaker says, “She don't know,” do not silently change it to “She doesn't know” in a strict verbatim transcript. Accuracy outranks your inner English teacher.
Lesson 05

Unclear speech and difficult audio

  • Unclear word: Replay the relevant audio in context before marking it unclear.
  • Inaudible speech: Use the client's exact notation, such as [inaudible 00:01:12], only when speech cannot reasonably be recovered.
  • Overlapping speech: Follow the required speaker-label and overlap convention. Do not merge two speakers into one sentence.
  • Unknown names: Check the provided materials or spelling references when permitted. Do not invent a spelling based on a guess.
  • Numbers and dates: Apply the client's number style consistently and verify each digit against the recording.
  • Accent or unfamiliar terminology: Listen to the whole sentence and use context as a clue, not as permission to invent words.
Do not confuse “unclear” with “I have not replayed it enough.” Make a reasonable effort first, then use the required notation honestly.
Worked example

Sample transcript

This short training example is illustrative. The speaker and recording are fictional; use it to study layout, labels, and punctuation rather than as a claim about a real recording.

Transcript title: Project Meeting: Weekly Update Client ID: PTW-DEMO-001 Company: PrimeTask Workspace Transcriber: Training Sample Duration: 00:00:24 Date: 12 October 2026 Speaker 1: [00:00:00 --> 00:00:07] Good morning, everyone. Um, let's start with the weekly update. Speaker 2: [00:00:08 --> 00:00:15] I finished the first draft yesterday, but I, I still need to check the figures. Speaker 1: [00:00:16 --> 00:00:24] Thanks. Please review the figures before Friday, and let the team know if anything is unclear. END OF TRANSCRIPT Duration: 00:00:24 © 2026 PrimeTask Workspace. All Rights Reserved.

Why this example works

  • It contains a clear header with a demo ID, not a real client's details.
  • Speaker labels stay consistent.
  • The filler “Um” and repeated “I” remain because this example demonstrates full verbatim.
  • Timestamps follow the same pattern and the footer is separated from the spoken content.
In a real job, derive timestamps and duration from the source media. Never copy demo times into a client's transcript.
Quality control

Common mistakes to avoid

Accuracy mistakes

  • Guessing words instead of replaying audio.
  • Removing repetitions in strict verbatim.
  • Adding a sentence that sounds logical but was never spoken.
  • Mislabeling speakers or confusing similar voices.

Presentation mistakes

  • Inconsistent timestamps or speaker labels.
  • Missing words at the start or end of a clip.
  • Random capitalization and punctuation.
  • Leaving out required headers, duration, or footer.
