Lesson 01 · The basics

What is audio transcription?

Audio transcription is the process of converting spoken words in a recording into written text. Recordings may contain interviews, meetings, lessons, phone calls, or other conversations.

A good transcript preserves the speaker's intended words while making the text easy to read. Accuracy comes first. Speed improves with practice, not with guessing what someone probably said.

Example

Audio: “We’ll meet on Thursday at half past ten.”

Transcript: We’ll meet on Thursday at 10:30.

💡
Remember

Follow the project instructions. Some clients want every spoken word preserved; others specify a clean-read style. Never silently change the meaning.

Lesson 02 · Listening technique

Listen in focused passes

Trying to hear, type, punctuate, and proofread everything at once is a lovely way to miss words. Separate the work into manageable passes.

1. First pass: understand the context

Listen to the full clip without stopping. Identify the topic, number of speakers, tone, and any difficult sections.

2. Second pass: draft the words

Type what you hear. Pause or replay short sections when needed. Do not invent a word just because the sentence would sound tidier with it.

3. Final pass: verify details

Check names, numbers, dates, contractions, speaker changes, and punctuation. Revisit unclear phrases before submitting.

Lesson 03 · Transcript standards

Punctuation, speakers, and unclear audio

Punctuation helps readers follow the speaker’s meaning, but it should not rewrite what the speaker said.

  • Sentence boundaries: use punctuation to show complete thoughts and natural pauses.
  • Speaker labels: when multiple people speak, use consistent labels such as “Speaker 1:” and “Speaker 2:” unless the instructions specify names.
  • Unclear words: replay the section and use the project’s required unclear-audio label if the words cannot be identified.
  • Numbers and names: follow the required style guide and verify spellings whenever reliable references are available.
  • Confidentiality: treat recordings and transcripts as private work materials.
Speaker-label example

Speaker 1: Have you sent the updated report?

Speaker 2: Not yet. I’ll send it this afternoon.

Self-check tip

Before you submit

Your quality-control checklist

  • Every audible word has been checked against the recording.
  • Names, dates, amounts, and times have been verified.
  • Speaker labels and formatting are consistent.
  • Unclear sections follow the project’s instructions.
  • The transcript follows the requested style guide and confidentiality rules.
